What the cleanup does

Every line tied to the job the bill names, or flagged

Jobs, as written

  • A job is kept only when its name or number is written on the bill. A job is never picked from the vendor's name or from the bill next to it.
  • Two jobs on one invoice? Each line keeps its own job.
  • A line with no job written is marked missing, and the report tells you to assign it by hand.
  • Jobs are grouped only by a matching job number ("J-118 Oakview addition" = "J-118"), PO, or identical text. A street number is never treated as a job number. Every grouping is printed so you can check it.

Amounts, as written

  • Each line gets a cost type: materials, labor, equipment, subcontract, permit, freight, fee, or unclear.
  • A line like "(x10), 48.50" could be a unit price or a total. It is flagged and not multiplied out.
  • Cost by job adds up stated or computed line amounts only. Credits subtract. A job with an unvalued line is shown as "at least."
  • Totals stay in the currency of the bill. Nothing is converted.

What you send

  • Supplier bills, yard tickets, sub invoices, and credit memos as text, CSV, Excel, or pasted from email
  • Texts or notes about bills, even half-remembered ones

What you get back

  • A CSV file and an Excel file, one row per bill line, with vendor, bill number, date, amount, job as written, job key, where the job came from (the line or the whole bill), and cost type
  • A short report: cost by job, lines with no job, groupings made, bills still open, credits to apply, and lines that need a person

One number, two meanings

Before

PEX 1/2in coil (x10),48.50

After

PEX 1/2in coil (48.50 as written) · qty 10 · amount (blank) · needs review: unit price or line total?

$48.50 each is $485.00. $48.50 for all ten is $48.50. That difference belongs on a person's desk, not in a formula.
